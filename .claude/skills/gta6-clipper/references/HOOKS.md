# Text hooks: why a stranger stops and stays

A clip lives or dies in its first 2 seconds. The viewer decides to stop on what they **see**, and to stay on what
they **read**. The hook text is not a title or a label: it is a promise that something wild is about to happen.
(Condensed from the MIRFAN visual-text-hooks study: 41 viral reels at 1M to 35M views, plus 37 reels of one page where
the same footage got 12K or 1.35M views depending only on the text.)

## The rules

1. **Exaggerate, but stay true.** Write the most extreme TRUE version of what happens. "Shaq's son put an S-Class
   on his dad's tab & Shaq has no idea..." beats "Shaqir gets a new car". Never claim something the clip does not
   show: a hook that lies gets "clickbait" comments and kills the page.
2. **A person first, then the stakes.** Use a name a stranger knows or a role they understand: "Kai", "Shaq's son",
   "this streamer's mom", "a 12 y.o. kid". Not "he", not the game.
3. **Plant one question, never answer it.** The viewer should think "wait, what happens?" Hide the payoff behind
   "...", "THIS", "until", "& then". Never show or say the ending in the hook.
4. **Past tense, as a fact:** "put", "lost", "got banned", "found out". Not "tries to", not "reacts to".
5. **Add what the picture can't say in 2 seconds:** who they are ("Shaq's son"), the stakes ("$40K", "live in
   front of 80K people"), a conflict ("& his dad doesn't know", "his mom was watching").
6. **One number or a contrast** when there is a real one ("3 years", "100K viewers", "1 vs 5").
7. **6 to 12 words, 2 lines,** ending in "..." or one emoji (the `plate` style shows no emoji: end those in "..."). On screen for at least 3 seconds (5 is normal).
8. **The text adds, it never repeats** the first spoken line.
9. **No context labels.** "Hour 103 of the marathon", "Kai's GTA stream", "Day 3": these tell, they don't pull.
   Only add a `note` when a stranger would be lost without it, and make it raise the stakes, not explain.

## Fill-in templates that work for clips

- `[PERSON] [DID SOMETHING WILD] & [OTHER PERSON] HAS NO IDEA...`
- `[PERSON] WASN'T READY FOR WHAT [CHAT / HIS MOM / THE NPC] DID...`
- `[PERSON] [WAS ABOUT TO X] UNTIL [Y] HAPPENED...`
- `[PERSON] ASKED [Z] FOR [THING]... THIS HAPPENED 😭`
- `[PERSON] [LOST / WON / BROKE] [NUMBER] LIVE ON STREAM...`
- `[PERSON] FINALLY [DID THE THING] AFTER [TIME / FAILS]...`
- `NOBODY EXPECTED [PERSON] TO [DO THIS]...`
- `[PERSON] GOT [CAUGHT / EXPOSED / BANNED] FOR THIS...`
- `[PERSON]'S [MOM / DAD / GIRLFRIEND] WALKED IN ON THIS...`

## How to write one

Write 5 options from different templates, then pick by these two tests:
1. **Clarity:** does a stranger know who this is and what's at stake in one second?
2. **Curiosity:** do they now NEED to see what happens?
Read the winner out loud like you're texting a friend about it. If it sounds like an ad or a news headline, rewrite it.

## The first 2 seconds of picture

- Start on the most interesting frame you have: a face mid-reaction, the car, the moment just before the chaos.
  Never on a slow walk-in or someone saying "so...".
- Something changes by second 1 to 2: a cut, a punch-in, a picture card popping in on the key word.
- The hook text sits where faces are not: the white `plate` under the captions (story edits), or the `outline` title at
  the top (gaming clips).

## Which hook style

| Clip | Hook style | Captions |
|---|---|---|
| A story told across several moments (IRL, deliveries, pranks, family, podcasts) | `plate` (black capitals on a white box under the captions) | `story` |
| Gaming, rage, one big moment | `outline` (white bold italic, emoji at the end) at the top | `comic`, `punch`, `speedy` |
| A talking clip, advice, drama explained | `white` box at the top | `classic` |
